Tortoise of Honor · 100% true, we promise

Jonathan, Elder of St Helena

Jonathan is a Seychelles giant tortoise who lives on the island of St Helena in the South Atlantic. He's believed to have hatched around 1832, which makes him widely celebrated as the oldest known land animal. He has outlived several governors, and he still gets hand-fed by his vet. He likes bananas and cucumber. He has, by all accounts, never once been in a hurry.

Tale one · Crime

Gerald Gets Pulled Over

Officer Delgado had been on the force for twenty years, and he had never seen anything like it. The radar gun read 0.4 miles per hour. In a school zone.

"License and registration," he said, crouching.

Gerald, a box turtle of fifty-one, pulled in his head and legs. His hinged shell snapped shut like a briefcase.

"Sir, I can see you in there."

Silence. Then, faintly, from inside the shell: "I'd like to speak to my lawyer."

His lawyer arrived three days later. She was also a turtle. She had been walking the whole time.

The case was dismissed. The judge cited reasonable doubt and "the most adorable thing that has ever happened in my courtroom." Gerald still drives that route every morning. He leaves at 4 a.m. and arrives at noon. He has never once been late, because nothing in his life has a deadline.

Moral: you can't rush justice. Ask Gerald. Slowly.

Tale two · Sports

The Rematch

Forty years after the famous race, the Hare called a press conference.

"I demand a rematch," he said. "I was robbed. I was napping, and napping is a medical condition."

Every reporter turned to the Tortoise, who was standing at the back of the room. Well, arriving at the back of the room. He had started walking over two weeks earlier, when the press conference was first rumored.

"I accept," said the Tortoise. "On one condition. The finish line is the year 2150."

The Hare laughed. Then he did some math. Then he stopped laughing. Hares get about twelve years. Giant tortoises can blow right past 150.

"That's not a race," the Hare said quietly. "That's a will."

"Slow and steady," said the Tortoise, and began the long walk home. He got there in time for Thanksgiving. Of the following year.

Moral: never challenge someone who measures time in centuries.

Tale three · Real estate

Shelly's Housewarming

Shelly sent out the invitations in spring. Housewarming! Bring snacks! Dress code: shell-egant.

Everyone in the pond came. The frogs brought flies. The ducks brought bread, which nobody is supposed to eat, which everybody ate. The heron brought nothing and was asked to leave.

"So," said Bartholomew the bullfrog, looking around the empty mud bank. "Where's the house?"

Shelly pulled her head in, then popped it back out. "You're looking at it."

"It's… cozy," said Bartholomew.

"It's paid off," said Shelly. "No mortgage, no rent, no landlord. I've lived in it my whole life and I take it on vacation."

The frogs looked at each other. Somewhere in the reeds, a hermit crab who had moved four times that year quietly began to weep. The party went until 9 p.m., which for turtles is basically a rave.

Moral: home is where the shell is. Literally.

Tale four · Mostly true, actually

The Pond Under Glass

Every November, Marjorie the painted turtle says goodbye to the sky.

She sinks to the bottom of the pond, settles into the soft mud, and lets the ice close over her like a lid. Her heart slows to a few beats a minute. She won't take a breath of air for months, and somehow that's fine. Painted turtles are famous for it.

Above her, kids skate. Dogs skid. Somebody's grandfather falls down and pretends he meant to. Marjorie hears none of it. She is dreaming, as far as anyone can tell, about the first warm rock of April.

And then it comes. The ice thins and cracks. Light leaks down through the green water. Marjorie lifts her head, kicks once, kicks again, and rises toward a sky she hasn't seen since autumn.

She climbs onto her rock and stretches every leg as far as it will go. Nobody claps. Nobody needs to. It's the best day of her year, and she has had forty of them.

Moral: some things are worth waiting for. Turtles knew first.
